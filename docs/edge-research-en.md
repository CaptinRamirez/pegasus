# Edge research: what the evidence says about "confirming" a daily-close Donchian trend system on BTC/ETH perps

Consolidation of five evidence reviews (order book / order flow; derivatives positioning; options and volatility; on-chain, flow, attention, macro and calendar; systematic trend-following literature) for the system defined in `docs/strategy.md` sections 2.3 and 3.1:

- 55-day close breakout entry, gated by the 100-day MA (shorts at half size), funding-rate crowding filter (long only if 3-day 8h-equivalent funding <= 0.05%; short only if >= -0.03%), regime filter (no new entries in the "range" state).
- 2.5 x ATR(20) initial stop on the exchange; exit on 20-day channel close; 0.75% of equity risk per trade (0.5% first three months), notional capped at 10% of equity.
- Decisions only at the 00:00 UTC daily close; orders placed in the 0-2h after the close; expected 6-16 signals per year on BTC+ETH; target backtest sample >= 300 trades across BTC (from 2014), ETH (from 2017) and 8-10 liquid coins.

Grades are the reviewers' own (A = multiple peer-reviewed sources at the matching horizon; B = one peer-reviewed or several careful practitioner sources; C = thin, blog-level or horizon-mismatched; D = no supporting evidence or negative evidence). Nothing below is new evidence; where the five reviews disagree it is stated explicitly.

## 1. Executive verdict

**What raises expectancy at his horizon (days to weeks per trade)**

1. The trend signal itself, run slowly. Time-series momentum / slow breakout is the best-supported return predictor in the whole crypto literature (Grade A in two independent reviews: Liu & Tsyvinski; Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen; Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen; Lempérière et al.; Kang & Ryu 2026 finds slow 12-week-class signals beat fast ones in BTC). The 55-day entry sits inside the horizon band where Carver's breakout Sharpe plateaus (80-320 days ~0.77-0.79 vs 40d 0.57 and 10d negative). The implication is a parameter choice, not a new input: do not add 1-5 day "analyst" overlays, do not fine-tune 55 vs 60.
2. Preserving the right tail. Trend expectancy is carried by positive skew with a 30-45% hit rate (Grade A). Anything that raises hit rate by truncating winners (profit targets, tight stops, partial profit taking) lowers expectancy (Kaminski & Lo 2014; Faith; Clenow). His 20-day channel exit and 2.5xATR catastrophe stop are already the right shape; the evidence says leave them alone.
3. Volatility-scaled sizing (Grade A in both the options/vol review and the trend review). It raises Sharpe modestly and cuts drawdown and left-tail losses substantially; it does not change hit rate. His ATR-based notional formula already does this. The only documented refinement with evidence (Bongaerts-Kang-van Dijk, FAJ 2020, Grade B, equities) is *conditional* targeting: cut exposure only in the extreme top decile of volatility, otherwise leave the system alone. That matches section 2.3's "no extra halving by default; test > 1.5 as a backtest variable".
4. A slow direction gate (his 100-day MA; the literature tests 200-day SMA / 10-month SMA / 50>100 DMA, Grade B). Its documented benefit is halved drawdown and fewer counter-trend whipsaws, with CAGR roughly equal or slightly lower. It mildly raises hit rate by removing low-quality trades; it does not raise raw return.
5. One far-apart second sleeve (Grade B): a 20/10 fast or 250-500-day slow Donchian/TSMOM sleeve with its own risk budget adds diversification (Carver; Etienne et al. 2025 barbell result). This is a Sharpe/drawdown improvement, not a hit-rate improvement, and is smaller on a 1-2 coin book than on a diversified futures book.

**What only helps execution (a few basis points per side, never direction)**

- Order book depth and order-flow imbalance (OFI/TFI): Grade A for *contemporaneous* price impact over seconds, Grade A that the forecast dies within ~2 price changes and that by 15 minutes crypto shows mild *reversal* (~1.3 bp gross) after taker-driven moves. Use: walk the OKX book for your size before sending, use post-only limits a tick or two inside the spread, prefer passive entry after a taker spike, do not chase.
- Time-of-day: depth within 10 bp varies ~42% intraday (Amberdata); slippage is worst at 15:00 and 20:00 UTC and Monday ~21:00 UTC; algorithmic bursts at the top of each hour and quarter hour; OKX funding settles at 00:00/08:00/16:00 UTC; Deribit expiry noise 07:00-09:00 UTC on >90th-percentile-OI days; a small positive turn-of-the-candle micro-return at 0/15/30/45 minute boundaries. Net: decide at the close, place limits in the first hours (not the first minutes) after 00:00 UTC, avoid resting stops that can be swept in the expiry window. **The order-book review and the calendar review differ slightly on the first minutes after 00:00 UTC** (one says avoid them because of settlement and algo bursts, the other says enter at/just after the bar open to capture the turn-of-candle effect); both agree the effect is single-digit bp and irrelevant to which breakouts succeed.
- Funding as a holding cost: funding is strongly autocorrelated and next-period funding is predictable (Inan 2025); +0.01%/8h is ~11% p.a. drag. Budget it; consider the quarterly future when perp funding is extreme.

**What is noise at his horizon (do not add to the rules)**

- Liquidation heatmaps and "liquidity" heatmaps (Grade D): model estimates of OI x assumed leverage; vendors disclaim them; no out-of-sample hit rate exists.
- Bid/ask walls as support/resistance (Grade B that they are unreliable): ~31% of large crypto CEX orders are spoof-capable; headline walls get pulled.
- CVD divergence / taker buy-sell ratio as a daily direction confirm (Grade C): no forward test; one blog backtest matches buy-and-hold on a bull sample; the daily ratio is mechanically correlated with the same day's return.
- Long/short account ratios, top-trader ratios, estimated leverage ratio (Grade D).
- Fear & Greed, social sentiment (Grade D): caused *by* returns, no out-of-sample gain.
- 25-delta skew, VRP, gamma exposure, max-pain, term-structure inversion as *direction* signals (Grade C-D): BTC option order flow has zero return predictability at 1h/1d/5d (Alexander-Deng-Zou 2023); BTC VRP has the opposite sign to equities; backwardation is contemporaneous with the crash.
- Bollinger / volatility-squeeze "the breakout is coming and this way" (Grade C): low vol predicts higher vol, never direction; Bollinger rules fail data-snooping tests. Section 2.3's "compression < 0.7 makes breakouts more credible" is therefore a hypothesis, not a finding; its apparent benefit in a backtest will come from larger size via a small ATR, which the sizing formula already delivers.
- Volume / RSI / MACD confirmation, pullback or retest entries, fine-tuning the lookback: no support in 14,919-rule, 75,360-rule and 5,806-rule x 49-market studies; MA/MACD/momentum are the same weighted-average-of-price-changes family as the breakout (double counting).
- Day-of-week, weekend, funding-settlement, expiry-date direction effects (Grade D).
- On-chain cycle indicators (MVRV, NUPL, SOPR, Puell, Mayer) at a daily horizon (Grade C): four cycles, decaying amplitudes, thresholds chosen after the fact. Global M2 lead, exchange-reserve "supply shock", Tether printing: Grade D.

**Open items with real but horizon-mismatched or thin evidence (log, do not trade yet)**

- Aggregate signed order flow at weekly horizon (Anastasopoulos et al. 2026, Grade B in both reviews that cover it) — but the paper's series aggregates many venues and fiat pairs; the free OKX taker-volume proxy is single-venue and untested. See the disagreement note in table row 4/18.
- US spot ETF daily net flows (Lim 2026, Grade B): 15-month sample, mostly same-day impact; usable as a 5-10 day rolling-sum confirm/veto or size scalar for BTC longs only, post-2024 only.
- CFTC COT leveraged-fund position *changes* (Baur & Smales 2022; Shi 2022; Grade B): weekly, 3-day stale, structural break at the January 2024 ETFs (level of the net short is now a basis-trade hedge).
- Rich quarterly basis / carry z-score (BIS WP 1087, Grade B): predicts crash risk and short-side liquidations over the next month, not mean return; a size-tilt candidate.
- Extreme liquidation days (K33, Grade B): predict weak follow-through for ~30 days, not bottoms; a size-tilt candidate.
- Whale-cohort accumulation (Philly Fed WP 24-14, Grade B, ETH-centric): weeks-to-months soft confirm, contaminated by exchange/ETF wallets.

**Bottom line.** The evidence does not support an additive "confirmation score" of order-book, flow, positioning and options signals for a daily-close breakout. It supports (i) leaving the signal and exits alone, (ii) a small number of *size tilts* in extreme states, (iii) a disciplined execution checklist, and (iv) a logged research series to be evaluated only after a pre-registered test. The one rule already in section 3.1 that the evidence argues *against* is the funding crowding filter as an entry veto (see section 4, F1).

## 3. Anti-findings: popular beliefs the evidence does not support

Order book / order flow

1. "Large bid/ask walls are support and resistance." About a third of large crypto CEX orders are spoof-capable (arXiv 2504.15908); headline walls are pulled before price arrives (Binance April 2025); no study shows walls predict where price stops.
2. "Liquidation-heatmap bright bands are price magnets you can trade on a daily chart." The maps are OI x assumed-leverage models, vendors disclaim them, no public out-of-sample hit rate exists after 4+ years; only cherry-picked 5-minute hits.
3. "Order-book imbalance tells you tomorrow's direction." OFI explains contemporaneous moves over seconds (Cont et al. 2014), forecasts die within ~2 price changes (Kolm et al. 2023), and by 15 minutes crypto shows reversal worth ~1.3 bp gross; nothing survives to a daily bar.
4. "CVD divergence / taker buy-sell ratio confirms or predicts daily breakouts." No peer-reviewed forward test; the one blog backtest matches buy-and-hold on a 2021-2024 bull sample with no costs; the daily ratio is mechanically correlated with the same day's return.
5. "A move that eats a lot of depth will continue." arXiv 2608.21888: depth consumed conditions nothing; what matters is whether the move was taker-driven, which predicts mild reversal.
6. "Weekends are the thinnest, worst time to execute." Weekend average depth within 10 bp is ~4% higher than weekdays with lower slippage; the real troughs are weekday US-session transitions (15:00/20:00 UTC) and Monday ~21:00 UTC.
7. "Deeper book levels or a fancier ML model turn the book into a swing signal." Multi-level OFI raises short-horizon R-squared (55% -> 80%) but does not extend the horizon.

Derivatives positioning

8. "Extreme positive funding means a top is near, so fade or skip the breakout." Funding is a momentum readout (perp premium R-squared > 50% on past 4-month returns), stays elevated for weeks in real trends, and contrarian funding systems test at ~54% win rate standalone with negative net Sharpe in cross-section after costs.
9. "A liquidation cascade is the bottom, buy it." K33: +0.78% mean (median -1.1%) in the 30 days after top-5% liquidation days vs +5.9% unconditional; only ~a quarter were local bottoms.
10. "Liquidation heatmaps show where price will be pulled." (Same as 2; both reviews agree.) The one academic cascade study (arXiv 2608.03616) found no precursor that reliably warned of individual cascades.
11. "Long/short account ratio or top-trader ratio is a reliable contrarian signal." No published test; 2 of 5 extreme readings marked bottoms; vendors call it a lagging sentiment gauge.
12. "CME leveraged funds net short means hedge funds are bearish." Since the January 2024 spot ETFs the short is predominantly the hedge leg of basis trades; only changes and extremes carry information (Baur & Smales, pre-ETF sample).
13. "Rising OI into falling price is a textbook reversal/continuation pattern." The four-pattern OI-price divergence framework has no published backtest; only the generic information content of OI (Palazzi et al.) is supported, with no economic magnitude.
14. "Funding carry is free money to pair with directional trades." BIS: the futures leg at 10x would have been liquidated in over half of sample months; the carry Sharpe (6.45 over 2020-2025 per Borri et al.) turned negative in 2025.

Options and volatility

15. "Max pain is a magnet into monthly/quarterly expiry." The documented BTC effect is an intraday V-reversal around 08:00 UTC on >90th-percentile-OI days only; equity max-pain profits live in small illiquid stocks; 2024-2026 Deribit expiries settled far from max pain.
16. "Extreme put skew is a contrarian buy / call skew a sell for spot." No BTC study shows daily-to-weekly directional predictability; Alexander-Deng-Zou find zero return predictability for option order flow at 1h/1d/5d; the only backtested edge is selling the skew itself.
17. "High implied-minus-realised vol (VRP) is bullish, like in the S&P 500." Almeida-Grith-Miftachov find no positive VRP-return relation in BTC (negative on average).
18. "A volatility squeeze tells you a breakout is imminent and which way." Vol clustering predicts larger moves, not their sign; Bollinger/squeeze rules show no significant profit after data-snooping correction; vendor "70-80%" figures have no significance testing.
19. "Volatility targeting raises win rate." It changes size, not the signal; the benefit is drawdown/tail risk, and Cederburg et al. (JFE 2020) show continuous inverse-variance scaling underperformed OOS in 72 of 103 equity-factor portfolios; conditional targeting is more robust.
20. "DVOL/implied vol is a better vol forecast than ATR/GARCH for stops." IV alone explains ~32% of future realised variance vs ~65% for EGARCH; adding IV adds little.
21. "Term-structure backwardation marks the bottom." BTC backwardation is contemporaneous with the crash, lasts ~85 hours on average; IV slopes have no shown predictive power for returns.
22. "Negative dealer gamma tells you the direction of the next move." It links to higher volatility and intraday fragility, not to the sign of daily returns; crypto GEX assumes dealers are the short side, unverified on Deribit.

On-chain, flow, attention, macro, calendar

23. "Global M2 leads Bitcoin by 70-90 days." A correlation between two trending series; 180-day rolling correlation vs forward-shifted M2 swung between +0.95 and -0.90 since 2021; lag chosen after the fact; BTC decoupled from M2 in late 2025-early 2026. Grade D.
24. "Falling exchange reserves / negative netflow = supply shock, bullish." BTC exchange inflows have no significant forecasting power even intraday (arXiv 2411.06327); most of the 2024-26 balance decline was coins moving into ETF custodian wallets; Glassnode called the narrative "a meme".
25. "Extreme fear is a buy signal / sentiment leads price." FGI is Granger-caused by returns, not the reverse; no OOS gain.
26. "Weekends dump / Monday pumps" and other calendar effects. No weekend-weekday mean-return gap, no BTC calendar anomalies post-COVID; reported day-of-week effects flip sign across samples.
27. "Spot-led rallies are healthier than derivatives-led ones." Kaiko found rallies were not decisively spot- or derivatives-led; no study links spot share to forward returns; perp volume is structurally ~6x spot.
28. "Tether printing pumps Bitcoin." Griffin & Shams documented a 3-hour effect in 2017; Lyons & Viswanath-Natraj find no systematic issuance -> price effect; hours-scale at most.
29. "MVRV / Pi-Cycle / Puell will call the top." Amplitudes decayed every cycle (MVRV peak 5.88 -> 2.74), fixed thresholds stopped triggering; four cycles is no out-of-sample support.
30. "Options max pain pins price into expiry." (Same as 15; both reviews agree.) Post-expiry "4%+ moves within 72 hours" are unconditional BTC volatility.

Trend-system design

31. "Volume, RSI or MACD confirmation raises the hit rate of daily breakouts." No peer-reviewed or large-sample study finds it; oscillators are the same weighted-average-of-price family as the breakout; in the 14,919-, 75,360- and 5,806-rule x 49-market universes volume/oscillator classes never emerge as robust OOS; perp volume is contaminated by liquidations.
32. "A higher win rate means a better system." Trend expectancy is carried by positive skew; Sharpe-maximising breakout speeds have 30-45% hit rates; the tools that raise hit rate (tight stops, targets) lower expected return unless returns are strongly autocorrelated.
33. "Fine-tuning the lookback (55 vs 50 vs 60) adds edge." Best in-sample rules do not persist (Bajgrowicz-Scaillet; Zakamulin; Hudson-Urquhart); adjacent lookbacks are the same bet (correlation 0.84-0.90).
34. "A 200-day trend filter raises returns." The benefit is drawdown and volatility with raw CAGR roughly equal or lower; on BTC the filter is ~82% a proxy for being long in bull markets.
35. "Waiting for a pullback/retest after the breakout improves expectancy." No systematic evidence; short-horizon mean reversion exists only at minute-hour scales; Faith and Clenow attribute most trend-system underperformance to missed breakouts.
36. "Crypto trend following is a free lunch (hundreds of percent per year)." The 255%/yr result assumes negligible costs on hourly data and is not peer reviewed; corrected studies show the edge is risk-adjusted, decaying on BTC, regime-dependent (negative OOS in H1 2018; lagged B&H by 7.7% in the pre-ETF bull).

No review found evidence for an additive score of "analyst" signals that raises the hit rate of a 55-day breakout. What the evidence supports is a short, tiered set of rules. Tier 1 contains at most three testable changes; each must be backtested alone (section 5 of strategy.md: one variable at a time) and must beat the baseline on the pre-registered metric or be dropped.

### Tier 1 — the three backtests worth running (one variable each)

**F1. Funding crowding veto: ON (current rule) vs OFF.**
- Exact rule under test: section 3.1's veto (no long if 3-day 8h-equivalent funding mean > 0.05%; no short if < -0.03%). Alternative: no veto; funding enters only as a cost and as the carry-reduction exit (halve after 3 days > 0.1%).
- Why: rows 15-17 and anti-finding 8. High funding is >50% explained by past 4-month returns; it is the normal state of the uptrends the system is built to catch; contrarian funding signals test near coin-flip and negative after costs. The veto is the one existing rule the evidence argues against. The derivatives review does support funding as a *holding cost* (row 16).
- Expected effect if the evidence is right: veto OFF gives **more trades** (the skipped ones cluster in strong uptrends), **unchanged hit rate**, **higher average R and profit factor**, higher funding paid. Veto ON gives fewer trades and lower carry; if its average R on the skipped trades is not clearly negative, drop it.
- Backtest: 2019-today (BTC+ETH, then 8-10 coins), next-open fills, maker 0.02%/taker 0.05%, 0.05% slippage, funding charged per settlement on held notional using Binance/Bybit full-history rates (OKX keeps ~3 months). Report both equity curves with and without funding. Primary statistic: mean R and count of the trades the veto would have skipped, with a bootstrap CI. Also sweep the threshold 0.03-0.10% to confirm the result is not knife-edge.

**F2. Conditional extreme-volatility size tilt.**
- Exact rule: if at the signal close 20-day RV / 100-day RV > 2.0 (section 2.3's "crisis") or 30-day RV is above its 90th percentile over the trailing 2 years, enter at half the normal notional. Never alter exits. (Section 2.3 already defines the crisis state with "new entries halved"; this is the test it asks for, and the 1.5 threshold is the sweep variable.)
- Why: rows 21, 22, 46 (Grade A sizing; Grade B for extremes-only conditioning being more robust than continuous targeting).
- Expected effect: **same trade count, same hit rate**, **lower max drawdown and fewer >1.5R losses**, slightly lower CAGR, higher MAR. The ATR formula already shrinks notional when ATR is high, so the incremental effect may be small; if MDD/MAR do not improve, drop it (do not keep it "for safety").
- Backtest: compare MDD (in R), MAR, count of >1.5R losses and their contribution to MDD, tilt ON vs OFF; sweep the ratio threshold 1.5-2.5 and the percentile 80-95. Because MDD is a single path statistic, use the strategy doc's bootstrap of the R sequence (1000 resamples) to compare drawdown distributions rather than one number.

**F3. Crowding/aftershock size tilt (one combined flag, or pick one of the two components).**
- Exact rule: enter at half notional if either (a) a top-5% daily liquidation day (rolling 1-year percentile within one data source) occurred in the last 30 calendar days, or (b) the annualised quarterly basis z-score (1-year rolling, OKX quarterly minus index) is above its 90th percentile at the signal close. Longs only for (b) (rich basis predicts long-side crowding and short-futures liquidations). Never skip the trade.
- Why: rows 11 and 14 (both Grade B): rich carry predicts crash risk and liquidations over the next month; post-cascade 30-day follow-through is weak (+0.78% mean, -1.1% median vs +5.9%).
- Expected effect: **same trade count and hit rate**, **smaller average loss on failed breakouts entered in flagged states**, lower MDD; possibly lower CAGR because some flagged entries are the start of real trends. If flagged-state trades do not have materially worse mean R than unflagged ones, drop it.
- Backtest: needs Coinglass (or The Block) daily liquidation history and OKX/CME quarterly basis; both exist from ~2019, so the sample is smaller than F1/F2 and should be run on all 8-10 coins. Primary statistic: mean R and MDD contribution of flagged vs unflagged entries, bootstrap CI. Note the basis regime shift in 2024-25 (thresholds must be re-estimated; use percentiles, not fixed levels).

If all three pass, the combined position-size multiplier is simply 1.0 x (0.5 if F2 fires) x (0.5 if F3 fires), floored at 0.25, applied to the ATR-derived notional. This is a *sizing* score; it never changes direction or whether a trade is taken (except F1, which removes a veto).

## 5. Multiple-testing warning: how many filters a ~300-trade backtest can support

The arithmetic, using the strategy document's own numbers (per-trade R standard deviation ~2-3R; target expectancy > 0.3R; 300 trades):

- Standard error of the system's mean R at 300 trades: 2.5 / sqrt(300) = **~0.14R**. The whole system's expectancy of 0.3-0.6R is therefore only 2-4 standard errors from zero. There is very little statistical room left to subdivide.
- A *filter* is a comparison between the trades it removes and the trades it keeps. If a filter removes 30% of trades (90 removed, 210 kept), the standard error of the difference in mean R is 2.5 x sqrt(1/90 + 1/210) = **~0.32R**. To be significant at 2 sigma for a single pre-registered filter, the removed trades must average **~0.6R worse** than the kept ones — e.g. kept +0.5R, removed -0.1R or worse. Filters that remove only 10% of trades (30 trades) need a ~1R gap. Only coarse, high-impact filters are detectable; subtle "confirmation" effects of 0.1-0.2R are invisible at this sample size and will look real or fake at random.
- With k candidate filters tested on the same trades, the Bonferroni-corrected bar rises (k = 3 -> ~2.4 sigma -> ~0.75R gap; k = 5 -> ~2.6 sigma -> ~0.8R; k = 10 -> ~2.8 sigma -> ~0.9R). The parameter sweeps in section 5 of strategy.md (55 -> 40-80, 20 -> 10-30, ATR 2-4, efficiency ratio 0.1-0.4) are robustness scans and are fine as long as nothing is selected from them; the moment the best cell is picked, each cell counts as a test.
- The 300 trades are not independent: BTC and ETH are ~1.2 independent markets by the document's own estimate, and the alts share the same crypto beta, so the effective sample is closer to 150-200 and every error above is understated.
- *Size tilts* (F2, F3) do not change trade count, so they are judged on drawdown/MAR, which has even less power (max drawdown is one number per path). Use the bootstrap of the R sequence and ask whether the tilt shifts the drawdown *distribution*, not whether one MDD figure improved.
- The literature cited in the reviews is the warning in practice: in the 14,919-rule (Hudson & Urquhart 2021), 75,360-rule (Deprez & Frömmel 2024) and 7,846-rule (Bajgrowicz & Scaillet 2012) universes, the best in-sample rule does not persist out of sample, and Bollinger/squeeze rules vanish after data-snooping correction (Fang, Jacobsen & Qin). Hudson & Urquhart's best in-sample BTC rule — a channel breakout — went to Sharpe -0.05 in the first OOS half-year.

Practical rule: **pre-register at most three filters/tilts (F1-F3), each removing or re-sizing at least ~20% of trades, test each alone, in-sample 2019-2022 / out-of-sample 2023+, and require the effect to hold in both halves and across coins.** Everything else lives in the Tier 2 log until there is an independent reason (a new peer-reviewed result, or 50+ logged trades with a written hypothesis) to promote it. Adding a fourth filter does not add evidence; it adds a degree of freedom.
